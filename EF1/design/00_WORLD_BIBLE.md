# Emberfall — World Bible

*Canon reference for lore, regions, factions, and timeline. Built on the existing wiki lore (The Emberfall, The Bound Ember, The Wardens of Ash, The Seven Regions, The Failing Seals, The Wanderer, Factions) — nothing here contradicts it; everything here deepens it.*

---

## Part I — Cosmology: What the Ember Actually Is

### The Old Truth (what everyone believes)

A shard of living fire fell from the sky. It burned the world. Guardians buried it. That is the story children learn in Greenrest, and it is true the way a woodcut is true — the shape is right and every detail is wrong.

### The Deeper Truth (revealed across the questlines)

The Ember did not *fall*. It was **thrown down** — cast out of somewhere, by something, as a sentence.

The Ember is a **Hearth-Spirit**: one of the old world-fires that were set beneath the earth to keep the world warm from below. There were nine. Eight still sleep under mountains, seas, and ice, doing their slow patient work, and no one has ever needed to name them. The ninth woke up.

A waking hearth-fire does not want to destroy. It wants to be *tended*. It wants hands, and voices, and someone to feed it and speak to it and tell it that it matters. It reached upward for tenders and found people, and it did to them what fire does to anything it loves: it consumed them, and did not understand why they stopped answering.

**The Ember is not evil. It is lonely, immortal, and cannot be near anything living without burning it.** Every atrocity in Emberfall's history is a hearth-fire trying to make friends.

This reframes the entire setting. The Wardens did not cage a monster. They **buried a grieving child under three tombstones and told it to be quiet**, because they could think of nothing else to do — and because the alternative was the end of everything.

### The Three Seals

The Wardens sealed the door with three things they valued most. Each seal is a real, physical relic, and each has a keeper.

| Seal | Forged From | Location | Keeper | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Oath** | Every Warden's spoken vow, bound into a bell | Thornwood — the Verse Bell in the Singing Grove | The Choir of Thorns (Elder Willow's order) | Cracked. Rings flat. |
| **The Watch** | The unbroken vigil of the Warden sentinels, bound into a lantern-flame | Frostmere — the Wayfire under the glacier | Brother Edric's order | Guttering. Half the wicks are out. |
| **The Cinders** | The lives of the seven Wardens who chose to die to make it | Ashen Barrow — worn by the Ashen Warden itself | The Ashen Warden | Held. The only seal still whole. |

> *"Three seals became one relic. One relic became a door."*
> The Ember relic recovered in **Beneath the Ashen Barrow** is not the seal. It is the **doorknob** — the piece that lets a bearer choose which way the door swings. This is why Kessa wants it, and why Mira went pale when you handed it over.

### The Ember-Touched

Some people are born with the Ember's attention on them. They run hot, sleep badly, and fire behaves oddly in their presence — it leans toward them. The Wardens called this **ember-touched**, and every Warden bloodline carries it.

The Wanderer is ember-touched to a degree not seen in three centuries. The Ashen Warden did not strike you down at the door because **it recognised the family that made it**. That is the whole secret of your memory loss: you did not lose your memory in an accident. **You were unmade and remade at the door of the Barrow, and the part of you that knew why is still down there.**

## Part II — The Timeline

| Era | Event |
|---|---|
| **Before the Fall** | The nine hearth-fires are set beneath the world. The ninth is placed under what will become Emberfall. |
| **Year 0 — The Emberfall** | The ninth fire wakes and reaches upward. The night sky burns. Kingdoms that stood a thousand years gutter out in a season. |
| **Years 1–40 — The Burning Age** | The dead do not rest. Ash falls as snow across the whole continent. Refugees flee north to the ice and south to the marsh — this is why Frostmere and Sablemarsh were settled at all. |
| **Year 41 — The Binding** | The fellowship that becomes the Wardens of Ash drives the fire into the black hollow. Seven Wardens burn themselves into the Cinders seal. The greatest, **Warden Cass Ashvale**, gives up her body *and her death* to become the Ashen Warden. |
| **Years 41–300 — The Long Watch** | The order thrives, then dwindles. The seals hold. The land heals into seven regions. |
| **Year ~300 — The Silence of Verses** | The Choir of Thorns forgets the second half of the Oath verses. The Oath seal cracks. Nobody notices for a century. |
| **Year ~400 — The Frostmere Thaw** | The glacier shifts; half the Wayfire's wicks drown. The Watch seal begins to gutter. Cinderforge's volcano stirs for the first time in living memory. |
| **Year 441 — The Ashfall Spreads** | Ash begins falling again over the Wastes. Marsh-fever appears in Sablemarsh. |
| **Year 447 — Now** | The Wanderer wakes at the edge of Greenrest Vale with no memory. The Barrow tablets glow the same night. |

**Present-day framing for all quest text:** it is the seventh year of the spreading Ashfall, in the reign of King Aldric of Greenrest.

## Part III — Factions

### The Wardens of Ash *(existing — expanded)*
The fading order sworn to keep the Ember bound. Four known members remain: **Scholar Mira** (loremaster), **Captain Rowan** (northern watch), **Scout Vale** (Barrow door), **Guard Bren** (Vale).

**New canon:** the order has a fifth member nobody has seen in thirty years — **Warden-Commander Alder Ashvale**, Mira's teacher, who walked into the Ashfall Wastes to "check the seal" and never came out. He is alive. He has been sitting in the Wastes for three decades listening to the Ember talk, and he has come to believe it deserves to be let out. He is the true antagonist of the late main story; **Kessa is his student, not his equal.**

**Creed:** *"We are the ash that remains when the fire is done. We do not burn. We do not blow away."*

### The Emberwrights *(existing — expanded)*
Cinderforge smiths who harness the Ember's heat. **Torren** is the moderate face. Their true leadership is the **Deep Bellows** — three master smiths who work in the volcano's throat and have not come to the surface in years. They have begun forging things that should not exist: metal that stays hot forever, blades that remember being swung.

**Schism:** *Tenders* believe the Ember should be fed and appeased. *Quenchers* believe it should be smothered permanently, even if that kills the region's forges and livelihoods.

### The Choir of Thorns *(new)*
Thornwood's hunters, who keep the Warden verses alive as ballads without knowing what they are. **Elder Willow** leads them. They have been singing a load-bearing magical artifact for four hundred years and think it's a folk song. When the Wanderer restores the lost verses, they realise their entire culture is a maintenance ritual.

### The Quiet Court *(new)*
Sablemarsh's drowned nobility. When the Burning Age refugees fled south, an entire minor court fled into the marsh and never came out. They are still down there, still holding audience, still *polite* — preserved by the marsh-fever that is killing everyone else. They are not hostile. They are extremely bureaucratic. Getting anything from them requires satisfying **procedure**.

### The Ninefold *(new — endgame)*
A cult that has worked out the truth about the nine hearth-fires. They believe the correct response is to wake the other eight. Their symbol is nine dots in a circle, scratched on doorframes across all seven regions from the first hour of the game — foreshadowing nobody notices until Act III.

### New Frontier Regions

### 8. The Emberreach — *The Sky Wound*
**Level band:** 40–55 · **Raid:** The Fallen Star

The crater where the Ember actually struck, high in the northern mountains beyond Frostmere. Nobody goes there because there is nothing there — which is exactly what a four-hundred-year-old cover story sounds like. The crater is empty because **the Ember was never in it**; it burrowed. What remains is the *shell* it arrived in, and the shell is still warm, still humming, and still receiving messages from wherever it was thrown from.

**Sub-zones:** the Rim Camps (Ninefold pilgrims), the Glasslands (sand fused to mirror), the Shell (raid), the Listening Post (Warden ruin where they first heard the Ember speak).

### 9. Ninefold Deep — *The Other Eight*
**Level band:** 50–65 · **Raid:** The Second Hearth

A tunnel system running under all seven regions, connecting the ninth hearth to its eight sleeping siblings. The Ninefold cult uses it as a highway. Walking it takes you *past* the sleeping second hearth — and the cult is trying to wake it.

**Sub-zones:** the Long Dark (connecting tunnels), the Cult Warrens, the Second Hearth chamber (raid), the Weeping Stones' root.

### 10. The Ash Sea — *Where the Ash Goes*
**Level band:** 45–60 · **Raid:** The Cinder Galleon

Reached by ferry from the Long Docks. Four hundred years of ash blew east and settled on the water, forming a grey floating crust you can walk on — in places. A drowned merchant fleet is trapped in it, crewed by things that were once sailors.

**Sub-zones:** the Grey Crust, the Fleet Graveyard, the Cinder Galleon (raid), Gullwatch (a living port town on stilts, the only friendly settlement).

### 11. Ashvale Keep — *The Family Seat*
**Level band:** 55–70 · **Raid:** The Hollow Throne (final)

The ancestral home of the Ashvale line — the Wanderer's family. Hidden in a fold of the Thornwood hills, unfindable until the late main story reveals it. Empty for four hundred years and immaculately clean, because **something is still keeping house**.

**Sub-zones:** the Long Gallery (portraits of every Ashvale, including one of you), the Warden Archive, the Hollow Throne (raid), the Family Crypt (seven empty graves labelled with the Cinders Wardens' names, and an eighth marked with yours).

## Part V — The Central Question

Every questline in Emberfall circles one decision, and the game should never tell the player which is right:

- **RE-SEAL.** Repair all three seals. The Ember stays buried forever, alone, screaming quietly under the earth. The land recovers. The cost is that you have condemned a living thing to eternal solitary confinement — and the seals will fail again in another four hundred years.
- **RELEASE.** Alder's path. Open the door. The Ember comes up. It will burn everything it touches because it cannot help it — but it will finally not be alone, and it may be teachable.
- **TEND.** The hardest path, only unlocked by completing all seven regional raids and the Ashvale questline. Do not seal it and do not free it: **become its tender.** Take the Cinders seal from the Ashen Warden and put it on yourself. You become the next Ashen Warden — immortal, burning, and *present*. The Ember stops screaming because someone is finally down there with it.

The **TEND** ending is the true ending, and it costs the player character everything.

## Part VI — Style Guide for Writers

Keep every line of new content consistent with the game's Old School RuneScape register.

**Dialogue rules**
- Short paragraphs. Two or three sentences per chat box, maximum.
- Dry humour from ordinary folk; total sincerity from the Wardens.
- NPCs address the player as *"Wanderer"*, *"traveller"*, or *"ember-touched"* — never by name.
- Nobody explains lore in a lump. Information arrives in fragments, from people with agendas.
- Peasants complain about weather, taxes, and their neighbours before they mention the apocalypse.

**Chat colour conventions** *(matching the existing game)*
- `#ffd23f` gold — quest progress, level-ups, discoveries
- `#8fd0ff` blue — NPC speech, skilling success
- `#8fff8f` green — kills, cooking success, healing
- `#ff6060` red — damage, death, failure
- `#b8a888` grey — ambient flavour, examine text

**Examine-text rule:** every new object gets one line of examine text, and it should be a *joke or a chill*, never a description of the obvious.

> *Verse Bell* — "It is still ringing. It stopped four hundred years ago."
> *Cabbage* — "A cabbage. The realm may end, but this is a cabbage."
